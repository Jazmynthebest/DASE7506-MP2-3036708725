# Selecting Reliable LLM Reasoning

A compact verifier combining learned candidate quality with arithmetic structure

Huang Leyi  |  Student ID 3036708725  |  31 October 2026

> 84.7966% full-test accuracy396 correct selections out of 467 questions; 156,002 bytes of inference assets; 4.35 seconds for fresh prediction plus independent scoring on the local CPU.

## Abstract

Agreement among candidate solutions is useful evidence, but several explanations can share the same mistaken interpretation. This project tests whether a small learned verifier can improve on answer plurality by combining fixed representations, within-question contrasts, and arithmetic profiles learned from training references. A shared linear stage feeds two compact gradient-boosted residual branches: one scores candidate evidence, while the other also compares an extracted calculation with the arithmetic structure expected from pooled question context. A fixed half blend is followed by mean pooling over identical numeric answers.

The final frozen method reaches 396/467 on the complete course test bank, compared with 323/467 for plurality and 392/467 for the compact branch. Controlled validation ablations show that both branch combination and answer pooling add two correct selections. These gains are modest, and the method is a final-answer selector rather than a proof checker. The report therefore examines failures, uncertainty and reproducibility alongside accuracy.

# 1  Task and evaluation protocol

Each input question has four supplied candidate traces and a 1,024-dimensional fixed representation for each trace. The method returns one integer index. Correctness is determined solely by the unchanged course numeric parser and reference answer: any candidate with the correct final number receives credit, even if its intermediate reasoning is imperfect.

| Split | Questions | Disagreement questions | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Training | 4,755 | 2,114 | Parameter fitting / nested CV |
| Validation | 798 | 355 | Development selection / analysis |
| Test | 467 | 467 | Frozen final scoring |

A disagreement question has at least two distinct valid parsed answers. Training and validation also contain groups with one distinct valid answer; there are 443 such validation questions. The selector can resolve these by choosing a valid parse, so full-validation accuracy is easier to obtain than test accuracy. The report always labels the split and denominator.

## Training supervision and cross-fitting

A training candidate is positive exactly when its canonical numeric answer equals the training reference parse. All four candidates of a question stay together in every split. Three-fold and five-fold outer partitions use seeds 7506 and 7591, stratified by answer diversity and positive-candidate count. First-stage probes and arithmetic readouts for residual-model training are generated by an inner three-fold split with seed 7509. The outer-held question is excluded from every learned stage.

The residual rankers train on the 2,114 disagreement questions. Easier training groups remain available to fit pointwise embedding probes and the arithmetic readout. At final training, three-fold out-of-fold features train the residual stage; the shared probes/readout are then fitted on all eligible training inputs. No external data, pretrained weights or new generated solutions are added.

## Selection and final commitment

The compact baseline and arithmetic candidate were compared using training CV and validation. The half blend improves on compact in both recorded training partitions and on validation. Subsequent exploratory variants had to improve the three-fold screen, confirm on the fixed five-fold partition, and then improve validation before promotion. The final selected checkpoint was committed before its own test evaluation; the pre-test commitment and source/asset hashes are included.

Historical test scores of earlier frozen versions were already known. The final evaluation is therefore not described as a wholly unseen test throughout the project. Its role is to score the previously selected frozen method; the result did not trigger further fitting or tuning. Repeated development on the same training partitions remains exploratory evidence.

# 2  Shared evidence and compact ranking

## Input representation

| Feature block | Dimensions | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L2-normalized embedding | 1,024 | Candidate content |
| Embedding minus question mean | 1,024 | Within-question contrast |
| Absolute + centered text features | 280 | Answer validity, agreement, numeric/style evidence |
| Norm and similarity summaries | 4 | Relations to same/different-answer traces |
| Full representation | 2,332 | Shared linear ranking input |

The text block contains answer frequency, distinct-answer count, sign/magnitude/integrality, relations to numbers in the question, length/formatting statistics, lexical overlap and conservative checks of explicit arithmetic equalities. Formatting is retained as a possible signal, so this representation does not isolate mathematical semantics from writing style. Question IDs are used only for alignment.

## Small embedding probes and linear prior

Two embedding-only probes provide complementary scores: weighted binary logistic regression (C=0.1) and pairwise logistic ranking (L2=0.01). For each probe, retain its raw score, within-question centered score, mean for the same answer, mean for rival answers, and their difference. Appending these ten values to the 284 non-embedding inputs produces a 294-dimensional compact representation.

A separate pairwise linear ranker uses the full 2,332 features. Training pairs compare positive candidates against valid negative candidates, with equal total mass per question. A four-term calibration combines normalized full-ranker and probe scores with centered log answer frequency. Content coefficients are nonnegative; the consensus coefficient is bounded in [-2, 2]. Calibration uses training out-of-fold scores and L2=0.01.

## Answer-group objective

Candidates sharing one numeric answer have the same final-answer correctness. Let G be the set of valid numeric answer groups and s_bar(g) the mean candidate logit within group g. The residual models optimize the group cross-entropy below; duplicates do not create extra classes.

```text
L = log(sum_{g in G} exp(s_bar(g))) - s_bar(g_correct)
```

The compact residual model has 120 LightGBM trees, seven leaves per tree, learning rate 0.025, minimum leaf size 100, L2=10, feature fraction 0.8 and bagging fraction 0.85. The tree seed is 2026. It adds a small nonlinear correction to the shared linear prior. Tree count is fixed during the final comparisons; held-out early stopping is not used.

# 3  Arithmetic structure and fixed fusion

## What should a calculation look like?

Training reference derivations provide numerical equations. A restricted arithmetic parser maps source quantities to symbolic leaves and chains verified intermediate results. From each extracted program, a 22-dimensional target records operation counts, root operation, source-use/depth statistics and common constants. Reference programs are available for 3,880 of 4,755 training questions.

A Ridge readout (alpha=1000) predicts this target from the mean of the four normalized candidate embeddings. This mean is a pooled representation of the question context; it is not a separate pretrained question encoder. The readout is fitted only on parsed references inside the applicable training fold. It predicts a structural expectation, not a numeric answer.

## Candidate programs and compatibility

Candidate programs are extracted from their own numerical equalities and final answers. A checked fallback handles chained equalities and explicit percentages, retaining the original extraction when available. Numerically re-evaluating every accepted equality increases training candidate coverage from 7,338/19,020 (38.58%) to 11,613/19,020 (61.06%). This verifies local arithmetic, not the interpretation of the word problem.

For each candidate, concatenate the actual 22-vector, expected 22-vector, signed difference and absolute difference. Add five summaries: mean squared discrepancy, mean absolute discrepancy, maximum absolute discrepancy, cosine similarity and parse availability. These 93 features extend the compact inputs from 294 to 387 dimensions. Missing programs are represented explicitly; no candidate is rejected solely because extraction failed.

## Two complementary residual branches

The arithmetic branch uses the same residual training settings as the compact branch. Both share the embedding probes, full linear prior and calibration. This keeps the comparison focused on the added structural evidence rather than independent large ensembles.

```text
raw_j = prior_j + 0.5 * tree_compact_j + 0.5 * tree_arithmetic_j
```

```text
answer_score(g) = mean(raw_j for candidates j in group g)
```

Choose a candidate from the highest-scoring valid answer group; exact ties follow candidate order. The fixed 0.5 branch weight was chosen on training CV before its validation evaluation. No question-specific rules, answer lookup tables or cached evaluation predictions enter inference.

> Design boundary. A calculation can be internally consistent while omitting a condition. Arithmetic structure is therefore a soft learned feature. The final selector combines it with content and agreement evidence rather than treating extraction as a proof of correctness.

# 5  Ablations and rejected alternatives

## Fixed-checkpoint validation ablation

| Setting | All / 798 | Disagreement / 355 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compact branch only | 748 | 305 |
| Arithmetic branch only | 748 | 305 |
| Final blend without answer pooling | 748 | 305 |
| Final blend | 750 | 307 |

All rows retain the learned weights of the frozen branches. Removing either branch reduces the result from 750 to 748 correct selections. Disabling same-answer mean pooling also reduces it to 748. This inference ablation isolates the decision rule without retraining. It is post-freeze report analysis on validation and did not change the submitted model.

Pooling makes repeated answers share evidence rather than forcing a choice between equivalent final numbers based on one explanation. The result is two extra correct validation decisions; it does not establish that agreement or every pooled trace is logically reliable.

## Why a slightly better screening score was rejected

| Method / fixed configuration | CV3 | CV5 | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Frozen blend | 1,782 | 1,772 | Retain |
| Contextual pair comparator, 0.25 fusion | 1,783 | 1,767 | Reject |

A nonlinear comparator reads two candidates, their feature differences, shared context and textual/numeric contrasts. Both comparison orders are included in training, predictions are antisymmetrized, and rival numeric answers receive equal aggregation weight. Its one-question screening gain failed the locked five-fold confirmation. It was therefore not evaluated on validation or test and was not promoted.

## Broader development scope

Additional exploration covered explicit unit/quantity bindings, reference-language features, small attention models, pipeline resampling, alternative losses, rule-based arithmetic and algebraic sensitivities. Most did not exceed the training-CV screen. A conservative bootstrap blend tied rather than exceeded the baseline; changing the loss could help a minority subgroup while hurting total accuracy. These outcomes favor the smaller frozen design over additional complexity.

The accompanying experiment summary reports selected families, screened configuration counts and recorded costs. Earlier cached-stage timings overlap and exclude some preprocessing; they are not presented as a complete end-to-end search total. Repeated screening creates selection risk even when no test labels enter fitting.

## Correct minority: preserve the requested unit

A validation problem asks how many minutes faster a 4 mph runner finishes a 24-mile race than a 3 mph runner. Three candidates answer 2 and one answers 120. The selector chooses 120: 24/3 - 24/4 = 2 hours, or 120 minutes. This is behavior beyond plurality, although it does not prove which learned feature caused the choice.

## A remaining missed-condition error

A training out-of-fold failure asks about four train carriages, each holding 25 seats plus 10 extra passengers, across three trains. The selector chooses 105, omitting the four-carriage factor; the correct answer is 4 x (25+10) x 3 = 420. The selected multiplication is arithmetically valid but solves the wrong quantity. Among 332 training OOF errors, 245 have only one correct candidate, and the compact and arithmetic branches are both wrong on 313.

Correct answers can also mask faulty reasoning. In a validation plant-growth example, the selected trace reaches the correct 16 while describing an unjustified year-by-year doubling. The scorer properly awards final-answer credit, but such cases prevent interpreting the measured accuracy as stepwise reasoning verification.

# 8  Interpretation, limitations and attribution

## What the evidence supports

The strongest result is that learned candidate evidence substantially outperforms answer plurality within a small resource budget. Arithmetic profiles add complementary information: the fixed blend improves both recorded training partitions and validation over the compact baseline. The final score rises by four test questions. That is a useful bank-level improvement, but it is not evidence of a large or universally reliable reasoning gain.

The method has several limits. Program extraction covers only a subset of candidate traces and checks numerical consistency rather than full semantics. The pooled embedding context may already contain misleading candidate interpretations. Style features can correlate with correctness without representing reasoning. The two-branch blend remains highly correlated on errors, and final-answer supervision cannot distinguish an accidental correct number from a sound derivation.

Repeated development on a fixed public bank and reused CV partitions can overfit model choices. A separate instructor audit bank would provide stronger evidence of generalization. Future work should prioritize better linking of quantities to requested entities and units, and uncertainty calibration evaluated on a genuinely fresh split, rather than assuming that a larger ensemble will improve accuracy.
